# AI & LLM Software Development Regulations - New Zealand

**Last Updated:** 2026-10-11
**Tags:** #ai-regulation #new-zealand #privacy-act-2020 #software-development

---

## Overview

New Zealand takes a deliberately **light-touch, principles-based approach** to AI regulation. It was the last OECD country to publish a national AI strategy (July 2025) and has stated it will **not** introduce AI-specific legislation; that position held through 2026. Existing technology-neutral laws, primarily the Privacy Act 2020 and consumer protection frameworks, carry the load. Two binding privacy changes landed in 2026: **IPP 3A** (notification when personal information is collected indirectly) in force 1 May 2026, and the **Biometric Processing Privacy Code** whose grace period for pre-existing uses ended 3 Aug 2026.

## Key Policy Documents

### New Zealand's Strategy for Artificial Intelligence (July 2025)
- Title: "Investing with Confidence"
- Confirms the light-touch regulatory approach
- Focuses on AI adoption over AI development
- Aligns with OECD AI Principles (adopted by Cabinet June 2024)
- Estimates AI could add **NZD 76 billion** to GDP by 2038

### Responsible AI Guidance for Businesses (July 2025)
- Voluntary, practical guide for all NZ businesses regardless of size or industry
- Covers organizational considerations, AI governance, and AI system-specific risks
- Addresses cybersecurity, intellectual property, transparency, and risk management
- A further government "regulatory guidance" on AI was reported in June 2026 (unverified)

### Public Service AI Framework (January 2025)
- Guides responsible AI use across government agencies
- Led by the Government Chief Digital Officer (GCDO); a 2026 cross-agency survey is the only update found

### Responsible AI Guidance for the Public Service: GenAI (February 2025)
- Covers governance, security, procurement, staff capabilities, misinformation
- Incorporates ethics, bias/fairness, accessibility (including Maori and Pacific peoples), and transparency

## Existing Laws Applying to AI

### Privacy Act 2020
- The primary legal safeguard for AI systems in New Zealand; principles-based and technology-neutral
- **IPP 3A (Privacy Amendment Act 2025), in force 1 May 2026:** agencies that collect personal information indirectly (for example by scraping or from third parties, including for AI training) must notify the individual unless an exception applies
- **Biometric Processing Privacy Code 2025:** in force 3 Nov 2025; grace period for pre-existing biometric processing ended 3 Aug 2026; requires proportionality assessment, notice and limits on certain uses (binding code)
- The Privacy Commissioner has joined international joint statements on trustworthy AI data governance

### Customer and Product Data Act 2025
- Open banking regulations in force; ANZ, ASB, BNZ and Westpac designated first; electricity sector next
- Affects AI products that consume consumer data through designated data holders

### Consumer Protection (Fair Trading Act 1986)
- Prohibits misleading and deceptive conduct; applies to AI-generated content and recommendations
- Commerce Commission oversight extends to AI-powered business practices

### Human Rights Act 1993
- Discrimination protections apply to AI-driven decisions in employment, services, and government

### Copyright Act 1994
- No specific AI provisions
- AI-generated content copyright status is unsettled
- Text and data mining for AI training is legally ambiguous

## OECD AI Principles Alignment

New Zealand formally adopted the OECD AI Principles in June 2024. These guide how existing legal frameworks apply to AI:

1. **Inclusive growth, sustainable development, and well-being**
2. **Human-centered values and fairness**
3. **Transparency and explainability**
4. **Robustness, security, and safety**
5. **Accountability**

The principles do not create an additional regulatory overlay; they shape interpretation of existing law.

New Zealand's AI strategy incorporates **Treaty of Waitangi obligations**:

- Ensures AI development respects indigenous rights and perspectives

## What This Means for Developers

1. **No AI-specific compliance obligations** - but the Privacy Act 2020, IPP 3A and the Biometrics Code apply in full
2. **Indirect collection now needs notice** - if your model or product ingests personal information from third parties or scraping, IPP 3A applies since 1 May 2026
3. **Biometric AI** (face, voice, gait) must meet the Code; the grace period is over
4. **Voluntary guidance is the primary framework** - aligning with the Responsible AI Guidance for Businesses demonstrates good practice
5. **Transparency matters** - if deploying AI in customer-facing roles, disclose its use
6. **Bias and fairness** - discrimination laws apply to AI-driven decisions, especially in employment and services
7. **Maori data sovereignty** considerations are relevant for public sector work
8. **Copyright is ambiguous** - exercise caution with NZ-sourced training data
