# AI & LLM Software Development Regulations - Mexico & Latin America

**Last Updated:** 2026-10-11
**Tags:** #ai-regulation #mexico #latin-america #brazil #chile #colombia #peru #software-development

---

## Overview

Latin America is in the early-to-middle stages of AI regulation. Most countries are pursuing risk-based frameworks influenced by the EU AI Act, but legislative progress varies widely. **Peru** remains the only country in the region with a comprehensive AI law, and its obligations became operative in 2026 (regulation in force 22 Jan 2026; first mandatory phase 10 Sep 2026). **Brazil's** PL 2338/2023 is still stalled in the Chamber of Deputies, but Brazil now enforces binding AI-related rules through the children's online safety law (ECA Digital), the medical council and the securities regulator. **Mexico** enacted sectoral AI consent rules for performers (May 2026) and replaced its private-sector data protection law in 2025, while its general AI law stayed in committee. **Chile's** executive is rewriting its EU-style bill into an "enabling" framework law. AI is projected to contribute about 5.4% of GDP (approximately USD 500 billion) to the region by 2030.

## Mexico

### Current Status
- **No general AI law** as of October 2026
- Two parallel general AI bills (Zapata Bello, 223 articles; Ruiz Ruiz) were in committee in April-May 2026 with no vote; the Senate session closed without a dictamen on the Lagunes bill
- A constitutional reform to Articles 3 and 73 (needed to give Congress express AI competence; Monreal bill of 3 Feb 2026 among others) is pending
- **Enacted, sectoral (14 May 2026):** reforms to the Federal Labor Law and Federal Copyright Law published in the DOF require express consent for AI use of performers' voice and image, with fines of 1,000 to 5,000 UMA (secondary source; DOF text unverified)
- Sexual deepfake reform passed the Chamber of Deputies 17 Feb 2026 (442 votes) and is before the Senate

### Proposed Framework (general AI bills)
- **Risk-based classification** (aligned with EU AI Act and OECD principles)
- Creates a national AI commission as oversight body
- Mandates authorization, transparency, and accountability for high-risk AI systems
- Earlier drafts lacked an explicit prohibition on unacceptable-risk AI
- Alignment with USMCA digital trade principles for cross-border operations

### Existing Legal Framework
- **New Federal Law on Protection of Personal Data Held by Private Parties (LFPDPPP):** published in the DOF 20 Mar 2025, in force 21 Mar 2025. **INAI was abolished**; oversight moved to the Secretaria Anticorrupcion y Buen Gobierno. Applies to AI systems processing personal data.
- **Federal Consumer Protection Law:** Consumer rights apply to AI-powered services
- **Federal Copyright Law:** A 2025 Supreme Court (SCJN) ruling addressed AI-generated content but left the IP status of AI output unresolved; the May 2026 reform adds consent rules for performers' likeness
- Existing laws on discrimination, employment, and competition apply to AI use cases

### Copyright Uncertainty
- Whether AI-generated content qualifies for copyright protection remains unresolved after the SCJN's 2025 case

## Brazil

### Status: Comprehensive Bill Stalled, Sector Rules Live
- **PL 2338/2023** passed the Senate in December 2024 and sits in a Chamber of Deputies special committee (chair Luisa Canziani, rapporteur Aguinaldo Ribeiro); the rapporteur said on 24 Aug 2026 that a vote slips until after the **October 2026 elections**
- Most detailed proposal in Latin America

### Key Features of PL 2338
- **Risk-based classification** with "excessive risk" (prohibited), high-risk, and general categories
- **Mandatory preliminary risk assessment** for all AI systems before market introduction
- Fundamental rights emphasis - transparency, explainability, accountability
- Algorithmic impact evaluations with detailed implementation methodology
- **ANPD** as primary regulator

### Binding AI-Related Rules Already in Force
- **ECA Digital (Lei 15.211/2025):** grace period ended 17 Mar 2026; implementing Decrees 12.880, 12.881 and 12.882/2026 published 18 Mar 2026; ANPD restructured as an autonomous agency from 8 Apr 2026; preliminary age-assurance guidelines 20 Mar 2026; sanctions from **November 2026**, formal enforcement **January 2027**; fines up to 10% of Brazil revenue (cap R$50M per infraction)
- **CFM Resolution 2.454/2026:** AI in medicine, in force 26 Aug 2026
- **CVM Resolution 246 (30 Jul 2026):** AI in capital markets
- **ANPD Regulatory Sandbox:** Cycle 1 started 18 Mar 2026; first monitoring report 2 Jul 2026; runs to December 2026
- ANPD opened a call on 1 Sep 2026 for its 2027-28 regulatory agenda (AI, biometrics) and is revising its sanctioning regulation (hearing 24 Sep 2026)

### New Bills (Sep 2026)
- PL 5.349/2026 (AI companions); PL 5.271/2026 (under-16 social media ban)

### Existing Laws
- **LGPD (General Data Protection Law):** Brazil's GDPR-equivalent applies to AI systems processing personal data
- **Consumer Defense Code:** Applies to AI in consumer-facing applications

## Chile

### Status: Bill Being Rewritten
- First National AI Policy published in 2021; updated policy released subsequently
- Bill 16821-19 (risk-based, EU-style) passed the Chamber in October 2025 and is in the Senate
- The Executive announced (May, July and August 2026) that it will replace the text through an *indicacion sustitutiva* with an **"enabling" framework law**: ex-post sanctions, regulatory sandboxes and mandatory insurance instead of ex-ante EU-style obligations
- Chile is considered a **regional leader** in the Latin American AI Index (CENIA)

### Key Features (original bill, now under revision)
- **Risk-based approach** with "unacceptable risk" prohibitions
- Requirements for AI developers, suppliers, deployers, and importers
- **Ministry of Science, Technology, Knowledge, and Innovation** oversees AI policy
- National Center for Artificial Intelligence (CENIA) supports research and governance

## Peru

### Status: Only Enacted AI Law in the Region, Now Operative
- **Law No. 31814** enacted July 2023
- **Regulation (D.S. 115-2025-PCM)** published 9 Sep 2025, in force **22 Jan 2026**
- **First mandatory phase from 10 Sep 2026** for health, education, justice, security and finance
- Authority: Secretaria de Gobierno y Transformacion Digital (SGTD) of the PCM
- A February 2026 bill to add sanctions is pending

### Three-Tier Risk Framework
| Tier | Description |
|------|------------|
| **Prohibited** | Manipulative practices, autonomous weapons, unauthorized mass surveillance, predictive policing |
| **High-Risk** | Enhanced oversight required |
| **Low-Risk** | Basic compliance requirements |

- Requires impact evaluations, transparency, and public registries
- Part of Peru's national digital transformation strategy

## Colombia

### Status: Active Legislative Process
- Current vehicles: **PL 043/2025 (Senate) / 324/2025 (Chamber)** and a government bill from MinCiencias filed as **PL 025-2026 (Chamber, Jul 2026)**
- CONPES 4144 sets the national AI policy
- Earlier bills (including Bill 059 of 2023) were archived or superseded

### Key Features
- EU-inspired risk-based classification with "unacceptable risk" prohibitions
- Emphasis on regulatory sandboxes and compliance obligations
- Creates a national AI council as advisory body under the Office of the President
- Self-regulation, ethics, transparency, and technological sovereignty as guiding principles

---

## Argentina

### Status: Sector-Specific Approach
- **No comprehensive AI law enacted** - deliberate strategy to create a less regulated AI ecosystem to attract foreign investment
- **AI Strategy 2025** - non-binding governance guidelines
- Multiple AI bills under consideration in Congress; no material change in 2026

### Distinctive Approach
- Positioning as a potential AI innovation hub with minimal regulatory barriers
- Focus on AI applications in public administration and judiciary (e.g., Prometea)
- Data protection law includes a right against automated decision-making by courts/administrative authorities

## Regional Trends and Patterns

### EU AI Act Influence ("Brussels Effect")
Most Latin American proposals adopted risk-based classification systems influenced by the EU AI Act (Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Peru). Chile's 2026 rewrite is the first explicit move away from the EU model toward an ex-post, sandbox-first design.

### Common Elements Across Jurisdictions
- Risk-based classification (prohibited, high-risk, low-risk)
- Transparency and explainability requirements
- Impact assessments for high-risk systems
- Human oversight obligations
- Data protection integration (all countries have comprehensive data protection laws)
- Rights against automated decision-making (Argentina, Peru, Mexico, Chile)

### Divergences
- **Mexico** regulates through sectoral reforms while the general law waits on a constitutional amendment
- **Argentina** deliberately pursues lighter regulation for innovation
- **Brazil** enforces child safety and sector AI rules without a general law
- **Peru** is the only country with enacted, operative comprehensive legislation

### Santiago Declaration
Adopted by Latin American and Caribbean nations, promoting ethical AI principles and regional cooperation on governance.

## What This Means for Developers

1. **Peru's phase-1 obligations are live** (since 10 Sep 2026) for health, education, justice, security and finance AI
2. **Brazil's ECA Digital** bites in November 2026: age assurance, minor protections and ANPD enforcement apply to any service minors can reach; medical and capital-markets AI have their own binding rules
3. **Mexico:** obtain express consent before using performers' voice or likeness in AI; the data protection regulator changed in 2025
4. **Do not wait for PL 2338** - plan Brazilian compliance around LGPD, ECA Digital and sector rules
5. **Chile's final shape is unknown** - the EU-style text is being replaced
6. **Data protection laws apply everywhere** - LGPD (Brazil), new LFPDPPP (Mexico), etc. cover AI processing of personal data
7. **Copyright is unsettled** across the region
8. **Regional consistency is lower than it looked in 2025** - EU alignment transfers to Peru and Colombia, less so to Chile and Mexico
9. **Cross-border operations** benefit from USMCA alignment (Mexico) and regional data transfer frameworks
