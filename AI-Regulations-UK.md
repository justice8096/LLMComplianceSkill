# AI & LLM Software Development Regulations - United Kingdom

**Last Updated:** 2026-10-11
**Tags:** #ai-regulation #uk #data-use-access-act #software-development

---

## Overview

The UK has **no enacted comprehensive AI law** as of October 2026. It relies on a **sector-specific, principles-based approach** delivered through existing regulators (ICO, FCA, CMA, Ofcom, MHRA, etc.) rather than a single AI statute. The King's Speech of 13 May 2026 contained **no AI Bill**; the government instead announced a Regulating for Growth Bill to give regulators statutory sandbox powers. The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (DUA Act) has introduced significant AI-adjacent reforms to data protection, including relaxed automated decision-making rules and new deepfake offences, and most of it is now in force.

---

## Regulatory Framework

### Five Core Principles (2023 AI White Paper)
Not currently legally binding, but regulators are expected to apply them sector-by-sector:
1. **Safety, Security & Robustness**
2. **Transparency & Explainability**
3. **Fairness**
4. **Accountability & Governance**
5. **Contestability & Redress**

The government has said it intends to introduce a legal duty on regulators to give "due consideration" to these principles, but no bill to do so has been introduced.

### No Formal AI Definition
The framework focuses on two AI characteristics rather than a fixed definition:
- **Adaptivity** - systems that learn patterns and make decisions not directly envisaged by programmers
- **Autonomy** - systems that take actions or make decisions without express human oversight

## Key Legislation and Developments

### Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (DUA Act) - Major Reforms

| Provision | Effective Date | Impact |
|-----------|---------------|--------|
| **Automated Decision-Making (ADM) reforms** | 5 Feb 2026 | Replaced old UK GDPR Art. 22; expands circumstances for automated decisions; reduces blanket prohibition |
| **Deepfake intimate images offence** | 6 Feb 2026 | Criminal offence for creating or requesting non-consensual intimate AI-generated images |
| **Scientific research broadened** | 5 Feb 2026 | Commercial research now qualifies; broad consent permitted for scientific research processing |
| **Recognised legitimate interests, purpose limitation, international transfers, PECR fines** | 5 Feb 2026 | Commencement order SI 2026/82 |
| **Data subject complaints duties** | 19 Jun 2026 | Controllers must have a complaints process (s.103 / Sch 10) |
| **Copyright and AI report** | Published 18 Mar 2026 | Government report required by s.136; see Copyright section |
| **ICO statutory code on AI and ADM** | Required from 12 May 2026 | SI 2026/425 obliges the ICO to produce a code of practice covering AI, ADM and children's data; code not yet published |

### AI Opportunities Action Plan (Jan 2025)
- 50 commitments for driving AI development and adoption
- 38 of 50 actions fulfilled by one-year review (29 Jan 2026)
- Includes dedicated AI growth zones, infrastructure investment, National Data Library
- Progress tracked via public dashboard

### UK AI Bill - Status
- **No AI Bill in the King's Speech of 13 May 2026** and none introduced as of October 2026
- Lord Holmes' AI Regulation Bill (Private Members' Bill) had its Lords second reading on 4 Jun 2026 without government backing; it would create an "AI Authority" and mandate AI officers but is unlikely to pass
- The government's stated focus is frontier-model safety through the AI Security Institute and sector regulators, not a horizontal statute

### Regulating for Growth Bill (Proposed, announced 13 May 2026)
- Would give regulators cross-economy statutory sandbox powers and put the AI Growth Lab on a legal footing
- Further detail published 8 Jul 2026; bill not yet introduced as of September 2026

### AI Growth Lab
- Advisory (non-statutory) offer launched June 2026 after the consultation that closed January 2026
- Cross-economy sandboxes to test AI innovations under modified regulations once the Regulating for Growth Bill passes
- Legislation for testing powers promised for autumn 2026

## Sector Regulators and AI

### Information Commissioner's Office (ICO)
- **AI and Biometrics Strategy** (June 2025): "Preventing Harm, Promoting Trust"
- Draft ADM guidance consultation closed 29 May 2026; final guidance due winter 2026
- Statutory code of practice on AI and ADM now mandated by SI 2026/425 (in force 12 May 2026)
- Engagement with 11 foundation-model developers on personal data in training
- Audits of five police forces' facial recognition deployments
- Monitoring agentic AI systems with autonomous capabilities

### Financial Conduct Authority (FCA)
- AI Live Testing cohort 2 (eight firms) running to end-2026
- Mills Review on AI in financial services published 6 Jul 2026
- Focus on algorithmic trading, credit scoring, consumer protection

### Competition and Markets Authority (CMA)
- Strategic Market Status designations under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act: Google search (conduct requirements in 2026 include publisher controls over AI use of content), Google and Apple mobile platforms (Google final commitments 1 Apr 2026; steering conduct requirement consultation 30 Jun 2026)
- Foundation models market monitoring continues

### Ofcom (Online Safety Act)
- Fined 4chan GBP 50,000 and GBP 20,000 with daily penalties (19 Mar 2026, confirmed 21 Apr 2026); 4chan is refusing to pay
- Investigation into X over Grok-generated sexualised imagery opened 12 Jan 2026 and ongoing
- Generative AI chatbots that let users share content fall within the Act's scope

### Medicines & Healthcare Products Regulatory Agency (MHRA)
- Call for evidence on AI in healthcare regulation (closed Feb 2026)
- National Commission into the Regulation of AI in Healthcare established
- Recommendations expected 2026

### Ofgem (Energy)
- AI governance guidance for energy sector (May 2025)
- AI Regulatory Labs for industry testing against existing regulations
- Technical sandbox under development

### Civil Aviation Authority (CAA)
- AI-led safety reporting tools being implemented
- Budget for AI-powered regulatory capabilities in 2026/27

## AI Security Institute (AISI)

- Established at Bletchley Park as the AI Safety Institute following the 2023 AI Safety Summit; renamed AI Security Institute on 14 Feb 2025
- Operates within DSIT on a non-statutory basis; no legislation to make it a legal entity has been introduced
- Budget about GBP 66M per year; had tested more than 30 frontier models by January 2026
- Conducts pre-deployment evaluations of advanced AI models under voluntary agreements with developers
- International collaboration with the US CAISI and the International Network for Advanced AI Measurement, Evaluation and Science

## Copyright and AI

This remains the UK's most contested AI policy area:
- Government consultation on copyright and AI closed in early 2025
- The s.136 DUA Act report and impact assessment were published **18 Mar 2026**: no new text and data mining exception, the opt-out model was dropped, no mandatory training-data transparency, and s.9(3) CDPA (computer-generated works) kept under review; a possible personality right is being considered
- The status quo therefore stands: training on copyright works in the UK requires a licence or an existing exception
- A Parliamentary Working Group on AI and copyright was established (June 2025)
- Creative industries continue to press for transparency obligations; no legislation is scheduled

The July 2026 change of Prime Minister did not alter the sector-led approach; no AI Bill was announced by the new government as of October 2026.

Executive instruments (orders, decrees, cabinet decisions, ministry measures, guidelines) do not expire when the issuing leader leaves office; they stay in force until revoked or superseded. The mandate-end date is the point at which revocation risk rises and is the date to re-verify every instrument listed above.

## Cyber Security and Resilience Bill

- Introduced to Parliament November 2025
- Updates the NIS Regulations framework (similar to EU NIS2)
- Expands scope to managed service providers, certain data centres, and large load controllers
- AI used in IT systems of relevant service providers must comply
- Still progressing through Parliament as of October 2026

## What This Means for Developers

1. **No single AI compliance law** - but the DUA Act and sector-specific rules apply now
2. **ADM reforms (Feb 2026)** expand automated decision-making rights - review your systems; final ICO guidance due winter 2026
3. **Deepfake generation** is a criminal offence - ensure generative AI tools have safeguards; Ofcom is actively enforcing against platforms
4. **Sector regulators are actively issuing AI guidance** - check ICO, FCA, CMA and your sector regulator
5. **AI Security Institute evaluations** remain voluntary for frontier models
6. **Copyright is settled for now**: no TDM exception, so license training data
7. **Do not plan around a UK AI Bill** - none is scheduled; watch the Regulating for Growth Bill for sandbox access
8. **Embed the five principles** voluntarily now
9. **Cross-border with EU:** If you serve EU customers, you must also comply with the EU AI Act
