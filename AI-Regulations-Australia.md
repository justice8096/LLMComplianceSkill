# AI & LLM Software Development Regulations - Australia

**Last Updated:** 2026-10-11
**Tags:** #ai-regulation #australia #privacy-act #software-development

## Overview

Australia does **not yet** have standalone AI-specific legislation, but the position shifted in 2026. The December 2025 National AI Plan had confirmed reliance on existing laws; on **15 Jul 2026** the Prime Minister announced legislated **Australian Standards for AI** (bill expected early 2027), an **Office of AI** in the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet, and a digital duty of care. A Joint Select Committee on AI reports by 30 Nov 2026. In the meantime, binding AI-adjacent rules are arriving: automated decision-making transparency under the Privacy Act commences **10 Dec 2026**, eSafety Phase 2 codes covering AI companion chatbots took effect 9 Mar 2026, and New South Wales enacted a work health and safety duty for digital work systems.

## Key Governance Frameworks (Voluntary)

### Guidance for AI Adoption (AI6) - October 2025
Replaces the VAISS. Sets out six essential practices for responsible AI governance:
- Accountability and governance structures
- Risk identification and management
- Data quality and integrity
- Testing, monitoring, and evaluation
- Transparency and explainability
- Human oversight and control

Two versions available: **Foundations** (for organizations starting out) and **Implementation Practices** (for more mature organizations). Supported by an AI screening tool, policy guide/template, and AI register template.

### AI Ethics Principles (2019)
Eight voluntary principles aligned with OECD AI Principles:
1. Human, societal, and environmental wellbeing
2. Human-centered values
3. Fairness
4. Privacy protection and security
5. Reliability and safety
6. Transparency and explainability
7. Contestability
8. Accountability

## Proposed: Australian Standards for AI (announced 15 Jul 2026)

- Legislated standards for AI developers and deployers, with legislation targeted for early 2027
- Office of AI in PM&C to coordinate policy; a digital duty of care for platforms
- Data-centre power and water obligations; creators to retain control over use of their works for training (TDM exception again ruled out)
- National Cabinet considered the package in August 2026; a consultation paper closed in early October 2026 (both secondary reports, unverified)
- Government statement of 29 Sep 2026 flagged **mandatory AI incident notification** legislation by end-2026 (secondary sources only; unverified)
- Joint Select Committee on AI: submissions closed 14 Sep 2026; report due 30 Nov 2026

## Existing Laws That Apply to AI

### Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) - Key AI Implications
- The primary data protection framework
- **Automated decision-making transparency (APP 1.7-1.9) commences 10 Dec 2026**: privacy policies must describe any computer program that substantially supports significant decisions, not only AI. OAIC final guidance published 30 Sep 2026.
- **Tranche 2 reform:** exposure draft Privacy Amendment (Personal Data Protection) Bill 2026 released mid-September 2026 (submissions closed 18 Sep 2026); introduces a "fair and reasonable" test; small-business exemption retained
- **Children's Online Privacy Code:** exposure draft 31 Mar 2026; OAIC must register it by 10 Dec 2026
- Maximum penalties: up to **AUD 3.3 million** for privacy interference; AUD 333,000 for infringement notice breaches
- The OAIC has published guidance on privacy in AI product selection and generative AI model development

### Australian Consumer Law (Competition and Consumer Act 2010)
- The ACCC monitors AI's impact on competition and consumer protection, with an enforcement focus on "AI-washing" (Feb 2026 statement; unverified)
- Misleading or deceptive conduct rules apply to AI-generated content and recommendations
- **Competition and Consumer Amendment (Unfair Trading Practices) Act 2026** assented 6 Jul 2026; commences 1 Jul 2027

### Anti-Discrimination / Human Rights
- The Australian Human Rights Commission addresses AI fairness
- Employment discrimination laws apply to AI-driven recruitment and HR decisions

### Online Safety Act
- **Social media minimum age** (16) in force 10 Dec 2025; the Online Safety Amendment (Strengthening Enforcement) Bill (Jun 2026) would raise penalties to AUD 99 million and extend powers to app stores and age-assurance providers
- **Phase 2 industry codes** registered 9 Sep 2025 and in force **9 Mar 2026**, covering AI companion chatbots and age assurance
- The eSafety Commissioner has acted against "nudify" AI apps

### Sector-Specific Regulation
- **Healthcare:** TGA regulates AI medical devices under the therapeutic goods framework
- **Financial Services:** APRA and ASIC oversight applies to AI in banking/insurance
- **Employment:** Fair Work Commission jurisdiction covers algorithmic management
- **Telecommunications:** ACMA regulatory expectations extend to AI in broadcasting/telecoms

## AI Safety Institute (AISI)

Announced November 2025 with AUD 29.9 million; established in early 2026 and reported to be testing frontier models by July 2026:
- Independent technical analysis of AI capabilities and risks
- Safety testing and monitoring of advanced AI systems
- Advice to regulators and ministers on emerging risks
- Member of the International Network for Advanced AI Measurement, Evaluation and Science

## Copyright and AI

- **No text and data mining (TDM) exemption**: ruled out by the Attorney-General in October 2025 and reaffirmed by the PM in July 2026
- Licensing consultation through the Copyright and AI Reference Group continues
- Copyright Amendment (Orphan Works) Act 2026 passed 1 Apr 2026 (unverified)
- AI cannot be an "inventor" for patent purposes (Thaler v Commissioner of Patents, 2022)
- No clear rule on whether AI-generated outputs are copyrightable

- **Work Health and Safety Amendment (Digital Work Systems) Act 2026** passed 12 Feb 2026, assented 18 Feb 2026
- Primary duty of care that workers' health and safety is not compromised by algorithms, AI, automation and online platforms; main duties await proclamation and SafeWork guidance

### South Australia
- Royal Commission into AI reported to have commenced 1 Oct 2026 with a report due 1 Jul 2027 (secondary source; unverified)

## Public Trust Challenge

- Only **30%** of Australians believe AI benefits outweigh risks (2025 University of Melbourne/KPMG study)
- **78%** expressed concern about negative AI outcomes
- Only **30%** believe current laws and safeguards are adequate

## What This Means for Developers

1. **A mandatory regime is now scheduled** - Australian Standards for AI legislation is expected in early 2027; align with AI6 now so the transition is small
2. **Privacy Act ADM transparency (10 Dec 2026)** - update privacy policies to describe automated and AI-assisted significant decisions
3. **Companion chatbots and minors** - eSafety Phase 2 codes apply now; the under-16 social media ban is enforced
4. **Consumer law applies** - AI-generated content must not be misleading; "AI-washing" is an ACCC focus
5. **Workplace AI in NSW** carries a WHS duty of care once proclaimed
6. **Incident notification** may become mandatory by 2027; build logging and reporting now
7. **Copyright caution** - no TDM exception; license Australian training material
